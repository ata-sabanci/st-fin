The framework

The geometry beneath
the price

ICS — the Isotropic Coordinate System — turns raw price into dimensionless geometry. Here is exactly how it is built, layer by layer.

Layer 1 · The coordinate system

Two axes, both dimensionless

A raw price chart has a problem: a $5 move on a $20 stock is enormous, but on Bitcoin it is noise. The same angle means different things on different charts.

ICS removes the units entirely:

vertical   y = log(price) / σ
horizontal x = bars / lookback

The vertical axis is log-price divided by volatility. The horizontal axis is bar count divided by the lookback window. Both become pure numbers — so an angle of 45° carries the same meaning on gold, on EUR/USD, and on a penny stock.

Why "isotropic"? The scaling holds for a given (σ, lookback) pair — it is a model convention that makes measurements comparable, not a claim that markets are physically uniform.

Layer 2 · Volatility

Why Yang-Zhang, not ATR

The vertical axis divides by σ. The quality of that σ matters enormously — and most indicators use a crude one.

The Yang-Zhang estimator is the academic gold standard for volatility. It combines three components:

Overnight gaps

close-to-open jumps, which gold and many assets show constantly

Open-to-close drift

the intraday directional component

Rogers-Satchell range

the high-low-open-close intrabar range, drift-independent

ATR ignores gaps. Close-to-close ignores intrabar range. Yang-Zhang captures all of it — the most accurate single σ available, which is exactly what a coordinate system needs.

Layer 3 · The anchors

Three levels, frozen in place

A lookback window scans recent bars and freezes three reference levels. They stay fixed while price moves — giving every triangle a stable origin to measure from. When price breaks the ceiling or floor, the framework resets and re-freezes.

↑

Ceiling

The highest high in the lookback window. The upper structural boundary.

◆

Center

The geometric mean — √(HH×LL) — of the freeze bar. The balance line.

↓

Floor

The lowest low in the lookback window. The lower structural boundary.

Layer 5 · Four metrics per triangle

Twenty lenses from five shapes

Each of the five triangles reports four independent measurements. Five times four equals twenty geometric lenses — each a continuous series you can read, smooth, and build rules on.

∠

Angle (θ)

Steepness of the move. Signed: positive trends up, negative trends down. Steeper means stronger momentum.

↕

Distance

How far price has travelled from the anchor in σ-units. Large means stretched, small means compressed.

△

Area

Time and price displacement combined. Expanding means the move is gaining weight; shrinking means it is fading.

⊙

Centroid

The balance point of the shape. Shifts in centroid often lead visible price turns.

Optional layer · Normalization new

Put everything on one scale

One toggle converts every series — geometric lenses, oscillators, volume, price — into a percentile rank between −100 and +100.

Why it matters: an angle in degrees and a distance in σ-units live on different scales. Normalized, they share one axis — so comparing a Ceiling angle to a Floor distance, or RSI to a centroid, finally becomes meaningful. The lookback window is shared with the triangle system.
